## What the evidence says

_What real usage showed: numbers if your tool has analytics, counted behaviour if it does not. Put the signal that matters on screen._

- **Primary signal:** Since 100% of people interacted with the 'Start this test' button on the dashboard, I believe the work I've done does validate my hypothesis. My hypothesis was: We believe a first screen that leads with one headline metric, a plain-language takeaway, and one recommended action will cause users to act on the dashboard instead of bouncing, for the people who should make decisions from it. We'll know we're right when bounce rate drops and the recommended action gets clicked."
- **What moved:** 100% of people who saw my live page went on to click 'start this test' and completed all subsequent actions
- **What didn't:** There was no recommended action on the 'Revenue' and 'Retention' tabs, which made those spaces feel like a bit of a dead-end as far as my hypothesis goes. They did nothing to support or help prove my hypothesis correct.

_Observed behaviour: reach I had peers outside the class check my link as I was not present for the live session. 2 people clicked around on my live website.; core action Both individuals clicked every surface they could and were very thorough with it. ## Peer feedback

I was not present in class so this feedback is from other peers who looked at my link. Most people found the sign-in to be a confusing friction point given the context (fake website). It was unclear if they needed a real email address/account to sign in or could use a dummy account. Beyond that, the other tabs on my website (Retention and Revenue) were expressed to be dead-ends....ie, it was unclear what the resulting action was supposed to be from those views, if anything at all. A lot of people liked the first page that they landed on, which had a clear CTA on how to action on the data.
