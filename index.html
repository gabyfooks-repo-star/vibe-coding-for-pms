The Dashboard Nobody Reads

A ticketing analytics dashboard that leads every page with one number, one takeaway and one recommended action, so decision-makers act instead of bouncing or exporting to a spreadsheet.

The headline it leads with: the mobile app closes 48.6% of purchases from 41.0% of referrals.

Open the live product Read the Living PRD

Strategy summary

What I built. Houselights' channel dashboard had twelve equal-weight charts and no answer: 60% of sessions bounced within 15 seconds, and 74% of users exported to a spreadsheet to think. I rebuilt it decision-first for the mobile app PM. Each page (Channel share, Revenue, Retention) opens with one headline number, a plain-language takeaway and one recommended action per region, and that action becomes a prefilled brief in one click. It started as a clickable prototype and is now a live React app on Supabase, with email sign-in, per-user briefs protected by row level security, and every displayed value coming from the database.

What I learned, and V2. When the answer is on the first screen, people act on it: in peer testing most testers went straight to the recommended action. They rarely explored filters or other views, and peers called Revenue and Retention dead ends until those pages got their own actions. I also learned that the bounce half of my hypothesis can't be judged without instrumentation I hadn't built. V2 would let people see the first screen before signing up, track bounce and action clicks against the 60% baseline, show each estimate as a range rather than one confident number, and send confirmed briefs into a real ticketing tool.

The M2 hypothesis, and what the data says

“We believe a first screen that leads with one headline metric, a plain-language takeaway, and one recommended action will cause users to act on the dashboard instead of bouncing, for the people who should make decisions from it. We’ll know we’re right when bounce rate drops and the recommended action gets clicked.”
Verdict: partly supported, not yet confirmed
Supported

The recommended action gets clicked

In peer testing, most people went straight to “Start this test” on the first screen. That is the behaviour the hypothesis predicts.

Not yet measured

Bounce rate drops

The product doesn't track sessions yet, so there's no number to compare with the 60% baseline. This half is neither confirmed nor refuted.

The evidence is behavioural, from a small group of peer testers rather than a statistically sound sample, and the data in the product is fictional. Event tracking for bounce and action clicks is specified in the PRD and is the first V2 item.

The journey

  1. 1

    The problem

    Decision-makers opened the dashboard, saw twelve charts and left. “It tells me what happened but never what to do about it.” Baselines: 60% bounce, 1.3 weekly sessions per user, 74% export to a spreadsheet.

  2. 2

    The hypothesis M2

    One headline metric, one plain-language takeaway and one recommended action on the first screen will turn bouncing into acting. Success means bounce drops and the action gets clicked.

  3. 3

    The clickable prototype

    A high-fidelity prototype of the core screens: Channel share, Revenue and Retention by region and date, chart detail pages, a calendar filter, loading, empty and error states, and the recommended action brief.

  4. 4

    The Living PRD and engineering handoff

    Problem, users and jobs, scope, requirements with acceptance criteria, and the events needed to test the hypothesis, honest about what was mocked. The prototype was refactored into a React + TypeScript codebase named after the PRD's screens. Read the PRD.

  5. 5

    The live product M5

    Deployed on Netlify and wired to Supabase: a schema with named types and foreign keys, email sign-up and log-in, row level security so each person sees only their own briefs, last-write-wins editing, pagination, and loading, error and empty states with a 10-second limit on every request. houselights-ticketing-dashboard.netlify.app

  6. 6

    Evidence and iteration M6

    Peer testing showed people go straight to the recommended action, and called Revenue and Retention dead ends. The sprint gave both pages their own recommended action per region, computed from each page's own data. Call: iterate, and measure bounce next.
